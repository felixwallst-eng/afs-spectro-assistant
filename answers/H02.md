## Recommended methods

- **Mid‑IR (MIR) – ATR probe (conditional).** Worth testing if the streams can be presented as a thin liquid layer (<50 µm) at the probe tip, because ATR gives high chemical selectivity for protein and fat [S01 Table 1 (p.6)] and works with opaque, highly scattering liquids [S01 p.16].
- **Raman – Raman probe (conditional).** Worth testing if the sample can be accessed by a probe that tolerates liquids, powders or solids, because Raman provides composition selectivity and is less affected by water absorption [S01 p.16; S01 Table 1 (p.6)].
- **Mid‑IR (MIR) – Cuvette (conditional).** Worth testing in a laboratory feasibility set‑up if a small volume of each stream can be placed in a transmission cuvette, allowing direct comparison with reference analytics [S01 Table 1 (p.6)].

## Measurement concept

Start with an immersible probe mounted flush to the pipe wall for the opaque milk concentrate (reflection/ATR) and a separate probe in a side‑stream of the clear whey (transmission or ATR) [S01 p.17; S01 p.5]. The probe(s) connect to the analyser via the appropriate fiber (UV/Vis/NIR or Mid‑IR) selected after confirming the required spectral range [S01 p.17; S01 p.5].

## Spectral range

Mid‑IR ATR and cuvette methods cover 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm) [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]; Raman uses 400–1100 nm excitation/detection (e.g., 785 nm) [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. The usable range must be confirmed against the available fiber link and any wavelength‑dependent attenuation in the process streams.

## Risks, limitations and uncertainty

- If the sample is a thick paste or solid, ATR or Raman probe may not couple efficiently (premise block).
- High turbidity could saturate transmission setups, making cuvette measurements invalid [S01 p.16].
- Mid‑IR fibres may not be available, limiting the use of ATR or cuvette methods.
- Variable bubbles or foam could cause scattering artefacts that degrade calibration [S01 p.17–18].

## First feasibility study

1. 1. Collect small‑volume samples of whey and milk concentrate at the intended measurement point.
2. 2. Measure each sample with a laboratory Mid‑IR ATR accessory and with a Raman probe, recording spectra under identical conditions.
3. 3. Perform reference protein and fat analysis on the same samples using the customer’s laboratory method.
4. 4. Develop chemometric models (e.g., PLS) linking the spectral data to the reference results and evaluate prediction errors.
5. 5. Compare probe‑based predictions to reference values and assess repeatability across multiple samples to decide if in‑line implementation is viable.

## Decision basis

A go/no‑go decision will be based on whether the probe‑derived protein and fat predictions meet the accuracy and detection‑change targets defined by the client (e.g., <5 % relative error and ability to detect the required concentration change). If the conditional methods satisfy these targets in the feasibility test, the corresponding interface will be selected for pilot deployment.
