## Recommended methods

- **Mid‑IR – ATR probe.** Worth testing if the samples are liquid, paste or soft and an in‑line probe can be installed, and a mid‑IR‑compatible fiber link is available; ATR works with highly scattering or opaque liquids without dilution [S01 Table 1 (p.6)][S01 Table 1 (p.6)][S01 Table 1 (p.6)].

## Measurement concept

Use a flush‑mounted reflection probe for the opaque milk concentrate and a transmission or ATR arrangement for the clearer whey; both probes can be linked to the analyser via appropriate fiber (UV/Vis/NIR or MIR) and positioned in the pipe or a bench‑top flow cell [S01 p.17][S01 p.18][S01 p.17].

## Spectral range

Mid‑IR nominal 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm) for ATR and cuvette methods; visible/short‑wave NIR 400–1100 nm for Raman. The exact fiber transmission capability must be verified for the chosen range [S01 Table 1 (p.6)][S01 Table 1 (p.6)].

## Risks, limitations and uncertainty

- Sample may be a powder, melt or rigid solid, making ATR or Raman probe unsuitable [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]
- High turbidity or opacity could saturate transmission setups, reducing signal quality [S01 p.16][S01 p.18]
- Mid‑IR fibers may not be available or long enough for the plant layout
- Probe fouling or coating by milk fat could degrade performance over time

## First feasibility study

1. Collect representative whey and milk‑concentrate samples at the intended measurement point.
2. Perform laboratory MIR cuvette measurements on each sample and correlate spectra with the known protein and fat values.
3. Set up a bench‑top flow cell with an ATR probe, measure both streams under flow, and record spectra.
4. Acquire Raman spectra with a probe compatible with liquids and powders on the same samples.
5. Analyse the data with chemometric models (e.g., PLS) and compare prediction errors and robustness across the three techniques.

## Decision basis

Proceed to full in‑line deployment if a method achieves a strong correlation (e.g., R² > 0.9) with laboratory protein and fat results, meets the required detection change, and demonstrates acceptable robustness to fouling and optical variability; otherwise reject or revisit the measurement concept.
