## Measurement concept

A fibre‑optic probe (ATR or Raman) would be positioned at the tissue surface to collect reflected or scattered light; the probe is linked to an analyser via a suitable fibre (silica for NIR, chalcogenide for mid‑IR) [S04 p.1; S04 p.4; S03 p.2]. Geometry and distance must match the chosen interface (contact vs non‑contact).

## Spectral range

Mid‑IR ATR and cuvette methods cover 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm); Raman uses visible/short‑wave NIR (400–1100 nm, e.g. 785 nm excitation). Confirmation of analyser and fibre transmission in the selected band is still required.

## Risks, limitations and uncertainty

- Clinical or in‑vivo use is outside AFS’s supported scope, so any recommendation is limited to laboratory feasibility [S03 ].
- A published ex‑vivo study does not guarantee performance on a new in‑vivo setup; validation data are mandatory [S03 ].

## First feasibility study

1. 1. Obtain ex‑vivo tissue samples that mimic the intended intra‑operative state (e.g., fresh soft tissue).
2. 2. Select a probe (ATR or Raman) compatible with the sample state and connect it to the analyser via the chosen fibre.
3. 3. Record spectra on the samples and on independent reference measurements (e.g., histology) to build a classification model.
4. 4. Evaluate model performance (accuracy, sensitivity, specificity) against the reference data.
5. 5. Document any deviations caused by turbidity, bubbles or temperature and assess repeatability.
