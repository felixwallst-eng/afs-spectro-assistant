## Measurement concept

An in‑process ATR probe (e.g., sealed ZrO2 or diamond tip) would be immersed in the petroleum line and linked to a remote MIR analyzer via a fiber cable; the current silica cable cannot transmit the required MIR wavelengths, so a polycrystalline, chalcogenide or hollow‑waveguide fiber is needed [S01 p.5; S02 p.13; S02 p.6].

## Spectral range

MIR ATR requires roughly 2.5–25 µm; silica fibers only cover 0.35–2.4 µm, so the usable range with the existing cable is limited to UV/Vis/NIR and must be verified for any alternative MIR fiber [S02 p.6; S02 p.8].

## Risks, limitations and uncertainty

- Silica fiber will block MIR transmission, making the concept infeasible unless replaced
- If the sample is opaque, turbid or contains bubbles, ATR or transmission may not achieve reliable contact or signal
- Unknown water concentration may be below detection limits of the tested methods

## First feasibility study

1. Obtain a small volume of the actual petroleum sample and determine its physical state and optical appearance.
2. Set up a laboratory ATR probe with a compatible MIR fiber (polycrystalline or chalcogenide) and measure water content using a reference MIR spectrometer.
3. Perform parallel Raman measurements on the same sample using a Raman probe and a cuvette setup to compare selectivity and signal strength.
4. Conduct NIR transmission measurements on the liquid sample to assess any usable signal in the 780–2500 nm range.
5. Compare all results against known water standards to establish detection limits and repeatability.

## Decision basis

The concept will be approved if a suitable MIR‑transparent fiber can be installed, the ATR probe provides reliable contact with the liquid petroleum, and the chosen method (Raman or NIR) can detect the required water concentration change as defined by the client.
