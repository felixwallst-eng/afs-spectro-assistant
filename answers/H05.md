## Measurement concept

An immersible fiber‑optic probe (ATR, Raman or NIR) placed directly in the liquid stream would provide in‑process sampling‑free measurements, as immersible probes are used for liquids in situ [S01 p.5; S01 p.10; S04 p.1]. The probe would be connected to the spectrometer via a suitable fiber link (silica for UV‑Vis/NIR, chalcogenide or fluoride for mid‑IR) of appropriate length for the plant layout. The geometry (ATR contact, transmission or diffuse reflection) will be chosen based on the optical variability of the process fluid.

## Spectral range

Mid‑IR ATR probes cover 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm) [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]; Raman probes operate with visible/short‑wave NIR excitation (e.g., 785 nm) within 400–1100 nm detection [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]; NIR transmission and diffuse‑reflection probes span 780–2500 nm [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]; UV/Vis probes cover the UV and visible region [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]. The exact range required must be confirmed once the target analyte is identified.

## Risks, limitations and uncertainty

- If the process requires non‑contact measurement, all probe‑based methods would be invalid.
- High turbidity or bubbles could invalidate transmission‑based probes.
- A mid‑IR fiber may not be available, limiting MIR methods.
- Without a defined target analyte, spectral region selection may be incorrect.

## First feasibility study

1. Obtain a small sample of the process liquid and measure its baseline optical properties (turbidity, absorption) with a handheld spectrometer.
2. Perform laboratory feasibility tests using a mid‑IR ATR crystal and a Raman microscope on the same sample to identify any characteristic bands.
3. Compare the laboratory spectra with reference measurements of known contaminants (e.g., standard solutions) to assess detectability.
4. If transmission is feasible, test a NIR transmission probe on the sample in a cuvette and record spectra.
5. Evaluate the signal‑to‑noise and detection limits against the expected concentration change; document the fiber type and length needed for each method.

## Decision basis

The final go/no‑go decision will be based on whether a probe can be installed in‑line, the spectral region that contains diagnostic features for the unknown contaminant, and whether the method meets the required detection limit for the expected concentration change.
